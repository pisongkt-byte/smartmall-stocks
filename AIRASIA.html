Rewards

Core demand is intact: average fare +21% YoY, load factor target 82%, 17 of 81 suspended routes already restored heading into Q4
Fernandes says AirAsia now has a SIGNED $1B term sheet with a Middle Eastern investor (up from just 'in talks' a week ago) — real progress, though he's still negotiating rate (targeting 7–8% vs current ~11%) rather than accepting as-is
Q2'26 EBITDA was still positive (RM442.6M) — the net loss is driven mostly by FX losses, interest expense and overseas-subsidiary losses, not a collapse in core operations
No aircraft repossessed or forcibly returned by lessors as of 27-Sep-26 — restructuring (route cuts, returning 25 older aircraft voluntarily) is proceeding without a forced event yet
Sister entity AAV (Thai AirAsia, separate financials) is meaningfully healthier — net D/E 1.1x, EBITDA breakeven, new ฿3.8B bond issued successfully — showing the regional network isn't uniformly broken

Risks

NEW (27-Sep): H1'26 filing confirms shareholders' equity is NEGATIVE (liabilities exceed assets by RM606M) — Smartmall raises bankruptcy/restructuring odds to 30–35% (from 25–30%) for 2026–27; heavy dilution / second scheme of arrangement held at 55–60% (sister Capital A already did PN17 in May 2026); unclear from public sources whether AirAsia Group itself is currently PN17-classified despite the negative equity — flagged as an open question, not resolved

Unhedged jet fuel exposure — Q2'26 averaged $183/bbl (+66%) with zero hedge, unlike AAV which hedged 13% of Q3 at $89; broader crude context (~$100/bbl per 24-Sep coverage) still pressures the cost base
A signed $1B term sheet is not closed funding — Fernandes is still negotiating rate (targeting 7–8% vs ~11% now) and media explicitly note 'a term sheet is not the same as cash in the bank'; refinance remains a hard deadline (Dec'26–Jan'27) — if it slips past Q1 2027 or oil stays elevated, Smartmall's own escalation rule moves odds of trouble to 40%+; a lessor lawsuit or aircraft seizure moves it to 50%+
Malaysian government already running contingency planning — asked Malaysia Airlines and Batik Air whether they could absorb AirAsia's domestic routes (Reuters, 16-Sep-26) — a signal authorities are hedging against a worse outcome even as Fernandes denies bailout talk
