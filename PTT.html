Risks

THE ENTIRE EARNINGS SURGE IS A TEMPORARY OIL-PRICE WINDFALL, NOT STRUCTURAL IMPROVEMENT. H1/2026 net profit +74.5% YoY and Q2 +143.9% YoY are driven almost entirely by the Strait of Hormuz closure (Brent $91.20 avg H1/2026 vs $71.93 avg H1/2025, peaked $118–150/bbl in March). FY2024 and FY2025 net profit were flat-to-declining BEFORE this shock. If the strait reopens and Brent reverts to the $65–75 range analysts expect post-resolution, E&P and refining margins compress fast and the stock's current 'cheap' 9.33x trailing P/E stops being cheap.
Price is sitting at the 52-week high (฿40.50 vs high ฿40.75, low ฿30.00) with essentially zero margin of safety against the base-case fair value range of ฿36–฿43. Buying strength right at the top of an oil-shock-driven earnings cycle is exactly the setup that produces the worst entries.
State-enterprise policy risk: the Thai government capped retail diesel at ฿29.94–30/litre via the Oil Fuel Fund (subsidy up to ฿18.31/litre), which ran a >฿12B deficit by mid-March 2026 burning ~$32M/day. PTT itself absorbed ฿8.5B of 'government-mandated energy subsidies' plus hedging margin calls in H1/2026 — as a 51%-MOF-owned SOE, PTT's windfall profit is a standing target for redirection toward consumer relief.
Q2/2026 booked a ฿21.7B inventory loss, showing how exposed quarterly results are to crude price direction — the same volatility that inflated H1 profit can just as easily erase a quarter's earnings if oil reverses sharply.
Petrochemical & Refining (PTTGC/IRPC) only just turned profitable after a loss-making FY2025 and remains structurally pressured by Chinese oversupply — a fast Hormuz resolution could send this segment back into losses quickly, unwinding one of the two current growth drivers.
PTT parent's own current credit rating was not independently reverified this cycle — only subsidiary PTTEP's AAA was confirmed. Do not assume the parent carries the same rating.
